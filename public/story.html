How Vari Fashion started

In 2019 a few of us stopped talking about starting a clothing label and actually started one. We already knew Tiruppur. A huge share of the clothes made in India get stitched there, mostly in big units turning out thousands of the same piece.

We wanted the opposite. A small room, a handful of machines, and people who would still be there next year. So we set up a family workshop in Tiruppur. Today it's around ten people: tailors, a cutter, and someone whose job is to say “no, do that seam again”.

We make things in small runs. If a style sells, we make a little more. If it doesn't, we learn something and move on. Nothing sits in a warehouse for years.

Finished pieces travel about an hour up the road to our shop in Erode. That's where most of our customers have met us so far. They come in, feel the cloth, try things on and tell us what they'd change. This website is how we bring that shop to people who can't drop in.

— The Vari Fashion team, Erode

How a piece gets made

p. 03
1

Cut in small batches

Fabric is laid out and cut a few dozen pieces at a time, so we're never sitting on a thousand of one shirt.

2

Stitched by people, not a line

Each piece goes through the hands of our tailors. Ten people, not a floor of three hundred.

3

Checked before it's folded

Seams, buttons and measurements are checked against the size chart. Anything that isn't right goes back to the machine.

4

Then to Erode, and to you

Finished pieces go to our shop, and online orders are packed by the same people who sell over the counter.
